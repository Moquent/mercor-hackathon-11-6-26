# Write-up (paste into the submission portal)

**RootProof checks an AI's incident root-cause analysis by replaying the outage with the AI's fix applied, instead of judging the prose.**

**Simulated incidents.** 15 seeded incidents on a fictional streaming backend: retries, timeouts, fallback chains, rate limits, multi-fault cases and a phantom outage. The subject AI (gpt-6-luna, a fresh request per run, five runs per task) answers from an on-call packet. TypeSafe Jev maps its prose onto 21 typed actions and its claimed causal edges. The actions are replayed from the page time, and an answer is accepted only if the mean user error rate falls below 5% in the judged window on seed 0 and a named root cause passes the timing, reachability, origin-versus-victim and baseline checks. Ground truth is computed by replaying every action and pair of actions. Results: 12 of 15 first attempts accepted, 58 of 75 runs accepted, 92% of runs agree with their majority verdict, 11 of 12 accepted fixes hold on all five simulator seeds, 30 of 30 on the verifier self-test, and 313 of 315 extraction decisions match hand labels.

**Investigation trail.** In a second track the AI investigates with tools (metrics, logs, health checks, connectivity probes, change log) against the same simulations, plus network partitions, DNS, TLS, proxy latency and packet-loss incidents. Every call is recorded with its arguments, the AI's stated reason, the output and the reasoning summary, along with every raw API response. Every claim in the final answer cites step ids. The verifier labels each claim: VERIFIED, UNSUPPORTED (the cited step doesn't show it), UNGROUNDED (it never looked), NOT_OBSERVED (the available tools did not observe a job), or FALSE (the simulation contradicts it). It also lists the true-path checks the AI skipped and pinpoints the first wrong step. Across 63 investigations (21 incidents × 3 runs, 22 tool calls on average), 529 of 542 claims are verified. In 9 runs the fix works but the trail has a false or unsupported claim. In incident 16 run 1 the AI correctly reverts a NetworkPolicy, but it never probes the link and claims "media-cache failures reach player-core". media-cache had 0% errors, and the skipped probe shows 92% of connections refused. A second hand audit of the extraction on runs 2–5 agrees on 418 of 420 decisions.

**Real telemetry.** RCAEval RE2 on Online Boutique: 30 cases per instance. The verifier checks the AI's claim without seeing the label: does the blamed service show an anomaly, does its metric signature match the claimed fault, did it degrade first, and is the claimed path a real call chain? On the fresh instance, which the rules never saw, it agrees with the label 90% of the time (95% interval 77–100%), and 91% of its flags are real errors. The tuned instance scores 87%; the small samples cannot rule out overfitting.

**Findings.**
1. The model fixes the trigger and misses the feedback loop. After a config change it reverts the change, but retries are already driving about 8x load, so errors stay at 100% in replay. In packet mode, tasks 02 and 13 fail on their first attempts; task 13 fails in every packet run.
2. Right fix, wrong story: in task 15 the actions work, but the blamed service degraded 30 seconds after users saw errors.
3. Its causal chains are precise (97%) but miss the load edges (58% recall).
5. A correct fix does not imply a correct diagnosis: with tools, 9 of 63 runs fix the incident while asserting something false or unshown along the way, and only the step-level trace check catches it.
4. On real data it always finds the service (97–100%) but mislabels the fault type about 40% of the time, mostly as "delay".

**Recorded event graphs.** The teammate’s instrumented order system contributes captured execution graphs and 12 prepared questions. RootProof checks dependency paths, retry and commit attribution, and trace completeness. gpt-6-luna answered 12/12 tasks correctly. On a separate set of 23 RCAEval trace tasks, it identified the injected service in 13/16 attribution cases and answered 7/7 reachability or gap tasks correctly. These small results are separate from the simulation and metric checks.
